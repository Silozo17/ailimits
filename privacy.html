Privacy policy

Effective 28 October 2026. This policy covers the AI Limits apps for Mac, iPhone, iPad and Windows.

The short version

We don't collect any data. AI Limits has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no server of its own. Your sign-ins and usage numbers stay on your devices.

Signing in to your AIs

You sign in to Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini inside AI Limits, on each AI's own sign-in page (claude.ai, chatgpt.com, accounts.google.com) shown in a built-in browser window. Your password goes only to that AI's provider; AI Limits never sees or stores it.

The sign-in is kept in the app's own browser storage on your device, separate from your normal browser. To refresh, the app opens the AI's usage page in a hidden built-in browser and reads only your usage figures there. Your sign-in never leaves that page. Signing out in Settings deletes that AI's sign-in and website data from the app.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings, such as which AIs and stats to show and the theme.
  • Your latest usage numbers, cached so the widget can show them straight away.
  • Your sign-ins, in the app's own browser storage, as described above.
  • A claude.ai session key, only if you choose to paste one instead of signing in. It's stored in your Mac's Keychain (Windows: Credential Manager).
  • A refresh log on Mac and Windows (~/Library/Logs/AI Limits.log, %LOCALAPPDATA%\AILimits\AI Limits.log) recording each refresh's percentages and any error. It never holds sign-ins.

Sign-ins the app can read from other apps

These are optional extras for people who use those apps. The app uses them only for an AI you haven't signed in to inside AI Limits, reads them where the other apps keep them, and doesn't copy or change them.

  • Codex: the Codex app's sign-in and usage logs, in the .codex folder in your home folder. The Mac App Store version reads it only after you choose that folder in Settings. The Mac direct download and the Windows app read it automatically.
  • Claude Code (Mac direct download and Windows only): Claude Code's login, if you've signed in to it. On a Mac it's in your Keychain; on Windows it's .claude\.credentials.json in your user folder. It's sent only to api.anthropic.com, to fetch your usage.
  • The Claude desktop app (Mac direct download and Windows only): the usage-limit entries Claude records in its own logs during Cowork sessions. The app uses them only when it can't get live numbers from Anthropic, and reads nothing else from those logs.

None of this is sent to us. Deleting the app deletes its own data (a Windows copy run from a folder rather than installed from the Microsoft Store leaves %LOCALAPPDATA%\AILimits behind; delete that too), apart from a saved session key (Mac: Keychain item; Windows: Credential Manager entry), which you can remove first in Settings. The other apps' sign-ins and logs belong to those apps and stay as they are.

Who the app talks to

To show your usage, the app connects to each AI provider's own website with your own sign-in, plus any sign-in service you choose on its sign-in page (such as "Continue with Google, Apple or Microsoft", or your company's single sign-on). Those pages may load their own content from other servers, as they do in your browser.

  • Anthropic (claude.ai, api.anthropic.com) for Claude
  • OpenAI (chatgpt.com, and auth.openai.com when you sign in) for ChatGPT and Codex
  • Google (gemini.google.com, and accounts.google.com when you sign in) for Gemini

The built-in browser is the one your system provides: WebKit on Apple devices, Android System WebView on Android, and Microsoft Edge WebView2 on Windows, which is covered by Microsoft's privacy statement. On Windows, Microsoft Defender SmartScreen checks page addresses in the sign-in window, as it does in Edge.

The app reads only your usage figures, never your conversations. Those websites are covered by each provider's own privacy policy, and while a page is loaded the provider may set its usual cookies in the app's browser storage. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google don't officially support third-party apps reading usage, so connecting an AI is at your own risk.

iCloud (Apple devices)

The Mac App Store version can also share your usage numbers with the AI Limits iPhone and iPad app through your private iCloud storage, for any AI you haven't signed in to on the phone. Only the numbers are shared, never sign-ins or session keys. The data is held by Apple under Apple's privacy policy, and we can't see it. You can turn this off in Settings → General → Show usage on iPhone.

Children

AI Limits isn't directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone.
